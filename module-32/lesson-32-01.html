Writing Your Own Music: Motives, Phrases, and Four-Chord Engines

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What makes a melody memorable?
Every original song starts with a small idea called a motive, and every motive lives inside a phrase. Once you understand phrase structure and have a chord engine under your melody, you have everything you need to write your first original melody.

🎵 PART 1 · MOTIVE AND PHRASE

A motive is the smallest repeatable musical idea: typically 2-4 notes that you could hum in one breath. A phrase is a longer musical thought, like a sentence, that usually runs 4 bars. The most satisfying phrase structure in pop and classical music is the period: two phrases that work like a question and answer. The first phrase (antecedent) ends on an unstable note; it sounds unfinished, like a question. The second phrase (consequent) ends on a stable note; it resolves, like an answer. Most verses and choruses you know are built from periods.

A period: two phrases in a question-answer relationship.

🎹 PART 2 · FOUR-CHORD SCHEMAS AS ENGINES

The I-V-vi-IV loop is a starting engine for a songwriter. You can play it on repeat while you improvise a melody on top. The chord progression gives your melody a harmonic home; the melody gives the progression personality. Modes add a different color: using the Mixolydian flat-7 (a lowered seventh degree) gives your melody a slightly bluesy or rock feel. The Aeolian mode (natural minor) gives a darker, more emotional feel often heard in pop ballads. You do not need to memorize mode names right now; just know that choosing a different scale color changes the mood of your song.

The chord progression is the engine. The melody is what you build on top.
⚡ TAP EACH CARD to reveal the answer:
The I-V-vi-IV loop as a songwriting engine: what does each chord feel like? (tap to reveal)
I (tonic): home, stable, at rest.
V (dominant): tension, wants to move forward.
vi (relative minor): slightly sad or longing.
IV (subdominant): warm, open, yearning.

The loop keeps cycling, which is why it can repeat under an entire verse or chorus.
What is Aeolian mode in simple terms? (tap to reveal)
Aeolian is the natural minor scale: it uses the same notes as a major scale but starts on the 6th degree. In C major that means starting on A: A-B-C-D-E-F-G-A. It gives a darker, more emotional color. Pop ballads and many rock songs use it.
✏️ PART 3 · COMPOSE AN 8-BAR MELODY

Activity: Compose an 8-Bar Melody

Do this in your notebook first: sketch the shape of your melody (up/down/flat). Then enter it in MuseScore.

  1. Open MuseScore (free at musescore.org) or use the MuseScore web app.
  2. Set the time signature to 4/4 and the key to C major.
  3. The chord progression for your 8 bars is: I-V-vi-IV | I-V-vi-IV (C-G-Am-F repeated twice). Write this in your notebook first.
  4. Compose a melody that lasts 8 bars. Try to give bars 1-4 a feeling of a question (end on a non-tonic note like E or G). Give bars 5-8 a feeling of an answer (end on C).
  5. Your melody should use mostly step-wise motion (moving by step: C to D, D to E) with a few leaps for interest.
  6. Export or screenshot your MuseScore file. Submit it in Canvas.

💪 THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER

A motive is a tiny musical seed; a phrase is the sentence it grows into; a period pairs two phrases as a question and answer. Put the I-V-vi-IV loop underneath, start humming, and you are already composing. Trust what you hear.
