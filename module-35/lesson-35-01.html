Composition Workshop: Planning and Drafting Your Original Piece

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
Everything you have learned this year -- scales, keys, chords, rhythm, form, style -- goes into a single project. Where do you start?
Composition is not mysterious. It is a sequence of connected decisions, each one building on the last. This lesson gives you a concrete seven-step process you can actually follow, so that by the end you have a plan and a working draft of your 16-measure original piece. The Q4 Composition and Analysis Project starts here.

🎵 PART 1 · THE SEVEN-STEP COMPOSITIONAL PROCESS

Composers do not sit down and produce a perfect finished piece from start to finish in one pass. They work through a process. The process has stages, and knowing the stages stops you from getting stuck. Here are the seven steps you will follow for your Q4 project.

THE COMPOSITION ROADMAP
1
GENERATE A MOTIVE
A motive is a short musical idea: 2-4 notes with a distinctive rhythm or contour. It is the seed of your whole piece. Hum something. Try it on MuseScore. Keep the one that feels right.
↓
2
CHOOSE A FORM
Pick a structure that organizes your 16 measures. Common choices from this year: binary (A A B B, 4 measures each) or 12-bar blues (expanded to 16 with an extra phrase) or verse-chorus (8+8). Your form is your blueprint.
↓
3
PLAN THE HARMONY
Choose a key and write out a chord progression using Roman numerals (I, IV, V, vi...) or a chord chart. Use one of the progressions you studied this year: I-V-vi-IV, I-IV-V-I, 12-bar blues, or a jazz ii-V-I. Write the progression for each section of your form.
↓
4
WRITE A MELODY
Write a melody that fits your harmonic plan. Use chord tones on strong beats. Add passing tones or neighbor tones between them. Aim for a shape: a rise, a peak, and a resolution. Sketch the contour in your notebook before entering notes.
↓
5
DRAFT IN MUSESCORE
Enter your melody and chords into MuseScore. Do not worry about perfection here. Get the pitches and rhythms down. This is a draft, not the final version.
↓
6
REVISE
Play it back. Ask: Does the melody have a clear shape? Do the chords support it? Is the rhythm interesting? Change what does not work. Add dynamics, articulations, or a second voice if you have time.
↓
7
NOTATE CLEANLY AND EXPORT
Clean up your MuseScore file: correct measure layout, proper clefs, key signature, time signature, and dynamics. Export as a PDF and an MP3 (or screen-record your playback). These are what you submit.

Notice that steps 1-4 happen on paper (or in your notebook) before you open MuseScore. Many students get stuck because they try to compose directly in the software. Plan first. Enter second. Revise third.

🎵 PART 2 · MELODY SHAPE: THE ARC

A good melody has a shape. It does not just wander. The most common and satisfying shape is an arc: the melody begins at a mid-range pitch, rises toward a high point (the climax), and then descends to resolve on the tonic or a stable tone. Think of it as tension building and then releasing.

THE MELODY ARC: RISE, PEAK, RESOLVE
Sketch your own arc in your notebook before entering notes. A rough contour drawing helps you keep the melody organized and moving forward.

The arc does not have to be perfectly smooth. Melodies can dip or jump and still follow an overall shape. What you are avoiding is a melody that starts high and stays high, or one that has no direction at all. The arc gives the listener a sense of arrival: something is going somewhere.

💡 MELODY CHECKLIST BEFORE YOU ENTER NOTES
  • Does my melody start on a chord tone (tonic, third, or fifth)?
  • Does it have a clear high point (climax), even if subtle?
  • Does it land on a stable note at the end (tonic or fifth of the key)?
  • Does it mostly move by step, with leaps used for emphasis?
  • Does it fit the rhythm of the style I chose (blues, pop, jazz, or tonal)?
🎸 PART 3 · WHAT STYLE WILL YOU WRITE IN?

Your Q4 project lets you compose in any style you studied this year. Each style has its own characteristic harmony and rhythm. Choose one:

BLUES
12-bar progression (I I I I / IV IV I I / V IV I V). Pentatonic or blues scale for melody. Characteristic blue notes (flatted 3rd and 7th). Shuffle rhythm optional.
POP
Common progressions: I-V-vi-IV or I-IV-V-I. Diatonic melody, verse-chorus form. Clear phrase structure (4+4 or 8+8 measures).
JAZZ
ii-V-I progressions. Seventh chords (Dm7 G7 Cmaj7). Swung or straight rhythms. Melody may include chromatic passing tones. AABA form (32 measures; scale to 16 by using 8-bar A).
TONAL (CLASSICAL STYLE)
I-IV-V-I cadences, diatonic melody, binary or rounded binary form. Strong on-beat harmonic rhythm. Melody stays mostly in the scale of the key, with occasional leading-tone or neighbor notes.

You do not need to pick the hardest style. Pick the one where you can make the most intentional decisions. A simple piece with deliberate choices is stronger than a complicated piece where you lost track of the harmony.

💪 THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER

Composition is a process, not a talent. Follow the seven steps: motive, form, harmony, melody, draft, revise, notate. Do steps 1-4 on paper before opening MuseScore. Give your melody a shape: rise to a climax, then resolve. You already know the theory. Now you use it.
