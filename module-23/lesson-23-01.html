Where did all the rules of tonal harmony actually come from?
Almost every concept you have studied in this course grew out of one historical era: the Baroque period (c. 1600-1750). The key signatures, the functional harmony, the figured bass notation, the four-voice chorale style: all of it was developed and codified during those 150 years. Today you meet the era and its master, J.S. Bach.

🎵 PART 1 · THE BAROQUE ERA (c. 1600-1750)
VISUAL · THE BAROQUE ERA IN WESTERN MUSIC HISTORY
The Baroque era is when tonal harmony, key signatures, and figured bass were systematized. Bach worked at its peak (1685-1750).

What made Baroque music sound like Baroque music?

The Baroque era in Western music runs from about 1600 to 1750. It followed the Renaissance and preceded the Classical era. During this time, major and minor tonality replaced the older modal system, key signatures became standard, and the rules of harmonic progression you have been studying were first written down and taught.

Baroque music has a distinctive sound built from several features:

Terraced Dynamics
Sudden jumps between loud (forte) and soft (piano) sections. No gradual swells. This was driven by the harpsichord, which cannot change volume based on touch.
Ornamentation
Trills, mordents, turns, and other decorations added to melodic lines. Performers were expected to add ornaments, especially on held notes and at cadences.
Counterpoint
Multiple independent melodic voices moving simultaneously, each with its own melodic identity. The defining structural principle of Baroque music.
Doctrine of Affections
A piece or movement should express one unified emotion (joy, sorrow, love, anger) from start to finish. Composers chose keys, rhythms, and intervals to match the intended feeling.
Basso Continuo & Figured Bass
A bass instrument (cello, bassoon) plays a bass line while a chording instrument (harpsichord, organ) improvises harmonies above it from figured bass notation. This is the harmonic skeleton of virtually all Baroque ensemble music.

Listen to Bach's Toccata and Fugue in D minor to hear terraced dynamics in action: search bach toccata fugue d minor on YouTube. Notice how the music jumps between sudden bursts of sound and quiet passages with no gradual fade in between.

🎹 PART 2 · BASSO CONTINUO AND FIGURED BASS

The harmonic skeleton of Baroque music

Basso continuo (Italian: "continuous bass") is a Baroque ensemble texture. A bass instrument (cello, bassoon, or bass viol) plays a written bass line while a chord-playing instrument (harpsichord, organ, lute) fills in the harmonies above it. The chord player reads figured bass notation: numbers below the bass notes that indicate which intervals to play above the bass.

This is figured bass: the bass note plus its numbers tells the chord player exactly which chord to realize.

You can hear the basso continuo texture clearly in Bach's cello suites: the solo cello carries the bass line, and you can imagine the chords implied. Search bach cello suite 1 on YouTube to listen.

🎼 PART 3 · J.S. BACH (1685-1750)

Why does Bach matter for music theory?

Johann Sebastian Bach is widely considered the greatest master of Baroque counterpoint. His output spans over 1,000 works: 48 preludes and fugues (The Well-Tempered Clavier), the Mass in B minor, six Brandenburg Concertos, six cello suites, the Goldberg Variations, and over 200 church cantatas. Every score is in the public domain and freely available at IMSLP (imslp.org).

For music theory study, the most important body of work is Bach's four-part chorales: over 370 harmonizations of Lutheran hymn tunes for soprano, alto, tenor, and bass. Each chorale is a perfect laboratory for studying voice leading, harmonic progression, and cadence. Music theorists have analyzed them for centuries to derive the "rules" of tonal harmony, including the rules you have learned in this course.

The Well-Tempered Clavier

48 preludes and fugues, one in every major and minor key. Proved that all 24 keys could be used effectively. It is the single most influential keyboard collection in Western history.
The Four-Part Chorales
(tap to reveal)
370+ harmonizations of hymn tunes. The definitive study source for tonal voice leading. Theorists have read them for centuries to describe how V resolves to I, how the leading tone moves, and how dissonances are handled.
Brandenburg Concertos
(tap to reveal)
Six concertos for different instrument combinations. Show how counterpoint can be built within the emerging concerto form. All public domain: free on IMSLP and YouTube.
Prelude in C Major, BWV 846
(tap to reveal)
The opening piece of The Well-Tempered Clavier. Consists entirely of arpeggiated chords moving through a tonal progression. A perfect example of how figured bass harmony generates a complete musical texture. Free on YouTube.

💪 THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER

The Baroque era (c. 1600-1750) is when tonal harmony was systematized. Its signature sounds are terraced dynamics, ornamentation, counterpoint, and the basso continuo. Bach's four-part chorales are the foundation of everything you have studied in harmonic analysis. Every V-I progression, every leading-tone resolution in this course traces back to him.
