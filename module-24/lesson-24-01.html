Elegance and Architecture: The Classical Period, Haydn, Mozart, and Sonata Form

📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What changed when the Baroque era ended, and how did composers use large-scale form as a design tool?
The Classical era reacted against Baroque complexity and chose clarity and balance. Composers built sonata form: a large three-part architecture that gives a movement the shape of a complete argument, with a statement, an exploration, and a return. This lesson explains the era, the two composers who defined it, and the form they perfected.

🎵 PART 1 · THE CLASSICAL PERIOD (c. 1750-1820)
VISUAL · THE CLASSICAL ERA IN CONTEXT
Classical (c.1750-1820) grew out of Baroque and led into Romantic. Vienna was its center.

What is the Classical style?

The Classical era (c. 1750-1820) reacted against Baroque complexity. Where Baroque music favored intricate counterpoint and constant activity, Classical music valued clarity, balance, and singable melody. The main texture shifted from Baroque counterpoint (multiple independent voices) to homophony: one clear melody supported by chordal accompaniment below.

Key features of Classical style:

Feature Baroque Classical
Texture Counterpoint (multiple equal voices) Homophony (melody + accompaniment)
Dynamics Terraced (sudden loud/soft) Gradual (crescendo, diminuendo)
Phrase Structure Continuous, flowing Clear, balanced (4+4, 8+8 measures)
Keyboard Harpsichord (fixed volume) Fortepiano (variable volume)
Large Form Suite, fugue, concerto grosso Sonata form, symphony, string quartet

To hear the Classical style, listen to Mozart's Piano Sonata K. 545: search mozart piano sonata k 545 on YouTube. Notice the clear, singing melody in the right hand and the simple broken-chord accompaniment below. Then compare with any Bach invention to hear how different the textures are.

🎹 PART 2 · SONATA FORM

The most important large-scale form in Classical music

Sonata form is a three-part structure used for the opening movements of Classical symphonies, sonatas, and string quartets. It is also called first movement form or sonata-allegro form. Think of it as a musical argument in three stages: state your ideas, develop and challenge them, then bring them home.

VISUAL · SONATA FORM STRUCTURE
EXPOSITION
Primary Theme
Tonic key (I)
Transition
modulates...
Secondary Theme
Dominant key (V)
DEVELOPMENT
Themes fragmented,
explored in new keys,
harmonically unstable
RECAPITULATION
Primary Theme
Tonic key (I)
Secondary Theme
Now ALSO Tonic (I)
Sounds like a statement
Sounds unstable, exploratory
"Coming home" - familiar, resolved

The key feature that makes the recapitulation feel like a resolution: in the exposition, the secondary theme was in the dominant key, which created tension. In the recapitulation, both themes return in the tonic. The tension resolves.

💡 THE KEY CONTRAST
Exposition: secondary theme in the DOMINANT (V). Recapitulation: secondary theme in the TONIC (I). That shift from V to I is what makes the recapitulation feel like coming home.

To hear sonata form in action, listen to the first movement of Mozart's Symphony No. 40 in G minor: search mozart symphony 40 first movement on YouTube. Try to hear: the first main theme, the moment it changes key, and the moment the main theme comes back at the recapitulation.

🎼 PART 3 · HAYDN AND MOZART

The two giants of Viennese Classicism

Joseph Haydn (1732-1809) and Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart (1756-1791) are the central figures of the Classical era. Both worked in Vienna (or for Viennese patrons), and together they define what "Classical style" means.

Joseph Haydn
1732-1809
  • 104 symphonies (a complete development arc)
  • Developed the string quartet as a major form
  • Famous for humor: Symphony No. 94 "Surprise" has a sudden loud chord in the slow movement
  • Symphony No. 45 "Farewell": musicians leave the stage one by one
  • Long court career let him experiment across decades
Listen: haydn symphony 94 surprise
Wolfgang Amadeus Mozart
1756-1791
  • Child prodigy; performing for royalty at age 6
  • 41 symphonies, 27 piano concertos, major operas
  • Operas: Don Giovanni, The Magic Flute, Marriage of Figaro
  • Known for effortless melodic beauty and formal precision
  • Piano Sonata K. 545: the most-studied Classical sonata form example
Listen: mozart piano sonata k 331

All scores by both composers are in the public domain and available for free at IMSLP: Mozart on IMSLP and Haydn on IMSLP.

💪 THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER

The Classical era (c. 1750-1820) replaced Baroque counterpoint with homophony and built large movements around sonata form: Exposition (themes stated, secondary in dominant), Development (unstable exploration), Recapitulation (both themes return in tonic). Haydn and Mozart are its masters. Every movement that "comes home" at the end owes something to this structure.
