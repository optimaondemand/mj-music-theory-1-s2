LESSON 25.01
The Romantic Period: Beethoven and Beyond
Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What happens when composers decide that rules exist to be broken?
By 1800, the neat, balanced world of Classical music was starting to crack open. Composers wanted more: more drama, more emotion, more color, more sheer size. The Romantic period (c. 1820-1900) is the story of what music sounds like when individual expression becomes the highest goal. Beethoven sits right at the hinge point, and understanding him is the key to understanding everything that came after.

🎵 PART 1 · THREE ERAS AT A GLANCE

You have now studied the Baroque and Classical periods. The Romantic period follows directly, and it keeps growing until the tonal system itself starts to break down around 1900. The timeline below places all three eras and highlights where the Romantic period fits.

Beethoven (1770-1827) is the most important transitional figure. He was trained as a Classical composer but his mature works pushed far beyond Classical norms. The diagram below shows how he connects both worlds.

Beethoven's Third Symphony ("Eroica," 1803) is often cited as the starting gun of the Romantic era. It was longer, louder, more structurally ambitious, and more emotionally raw than anything Classical composers had written. When Beethoven composed his Ninth Symphony in 1824, he was completely deaf and still producing some of the most powerful music in history.

🎹 PART 2 · WHAT MAKES MUSIC "ROMANTIC"?

Six features define the Romantic sound. Read each card below carefully; you will need these distinctions for the practice activity.

LARGE FORCES
Expanded orchestra: tuba, piccolo, English horn added; ensembles grow to 100+ players.
EXTREME DYNAMICS
Range stretches from ppp (nearly silent) to fff (shattering). Sudden contrasts are common.
CHROMATICISM
Frequent use of notes and chords outside the key. Creates color, tension, and emotional ambiguity.
PROGRAM MUSIC
Instrumental music that tells a story or depicts a scene (Berlioz's Symphonie Fantastique describes an opium dream).
NATIONALISM
Using folk melodies and national subjects to express cultural identity. Smetana's Ma Vlast depicts Czech landscapes.
INDIVIDUAL EXPRESSION
Music as deeply personal confession. The composer's own biography and emotional life become musical material.

On small screens the grid stacks to one column.

Key terms

Chromaticism is the frequent use of notes outside the current key. One signature of chromaticism is the augmented second found in harmonic minor. Romantic composers used chromatic harmonies constantly, which is why their music sounds emotionally restless compared to the more settled Classical style.

Program music is instrumental music attached to a story or image. A symphonic poem (or tone poem) is a single-movement orchestral work inspired by a literary or visual subject. Richard Strauss's Don Quixote even includes a bleating woodwind figure to represent sheep.

Lieder (singular: Lied) are German art songs for solo voice and piano. Franz Schubert wrote over 600 of them and essentially invented the modern song cycle as an art form.

💪 THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER

The Romantic period (c. 1820-1900) pushed music toward intense personal expression through a larger orchestra, more extreme dynamics, frequent chromaticism, and program music. Beethoven made the crossing first: trained Classical, composed Romantic. Every composer from Schubert to Tchaikovsky continued in the direction Beethoven pointed.
