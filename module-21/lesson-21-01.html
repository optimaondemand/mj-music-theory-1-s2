LESSON 21.01
Four-Note Chords: The Five Qualities of Seventh Chords
Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
What makes the dominant seventh chord the most powerful chord in music?
You have been building triads since early in this course. Now you add one more note on top -- a seventh above the root. That fourth note changes everything. It creates a dissonance that needs to resolve, and the most powerful of these chords pulls so hard toward the tonic that it drives the strongest resolution in all of tonal music. Today you learn all five seventh chord qualities.

🎵 PART 1 · FROM TRIADS TO SEVENTH CHORDS
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think about a song where the music feels like it HAS to resolve to a final chord -- like it is pulling forward. Have you ever noticed that feeling? (No wrong answer.)

A seventh chord is a four-note chord built by stacking a third above a triad. A triad has root, third, and fifth. A seventh chord adds one more: a seventh above the root. In root position on a staff, a seventh chord looks like four notes stacked on alternating lines and spaces -- an extra-long snowperson.

The seventh adds dissonance. The interval of a seventh from the root to the highest note is inherently unstable -- it wants to resolve. This is precisely what makes seventh chords so useful: they create energy and pull that drives harmonic motion forward.

A SEVENTH CHORD IN ROOT POSITION: C major-major 7 (Cma7)
💡 KEY TERMS
Seventh chord: A four-note chord built by stacking a third above a triad. Contains root, third, fifth, and seventh.
Chordal seventh: The seventh note added to the triad; distinct from the seventh scale degree.
🎹 PART 2 · THE FIVE QUALITIES OF SEVENTH CHORD

Seventh chord quality is determined by two factors: the quality of the triad (root + third + fifth), and the quality of the seventh from root to the top note. These combine to create five common seventh chord types.

FIVE SEVENTH CHORD QUALITIES: Comparison Table
Quality Triad Seventh Symbol Sound
Major 7 (ma7) Major Major 7th Cma7 Bright, rich, jazzy
Dominant 7 (7) ★ Major Minor 7th C7 Tense, must resolve
Minor 7 (mi7) Minor Minor 7th Cmi7 Dark, smooth
Half-dim 7 Diminished Minor 7th Cø7 Tense, uneasy
Fully dim 7 (o7) Diminished Diminished 7th Co7 Very tense, dramatic
★ = most important. The triad quality + seventh interval together determine the chord quality. The Dominant 7 row is highlighted because it is the focus of Part 3.
🎹 PART 3 · THE DOMINANT SEVENTH CHORD (V7)

The dominant seventh chord (V7) is the most harmonically important seventh chord in tonal music. It is always built on scale degree 5 (the dominant). It contains a major triad plus a minor seventh above the root.

What makes V7 so powerful? It contains a tritone -- the most dissonant interval in music -- between scale degree 7 (the leading tone) and scale degree 4 (the subdominant). This tritone resolves outward: the leading tone moves up a half step to the tonic, and the subdominant moves down a half step to the mediant. This V7-to-I resolution is the strongest harmonic motion in tonal music.

🎵 V7 IN C MAJOR
G7 (V7 in C major) = G – B – D – F
The leading tone is B (scale degree 7). It resolves UP to C.
The subdominant is F (scale degree 4). It resolves DOWN to E.
G7 resolves to C major (I). This is the most powerful resolution in tonal music.
🎹 PART 4 · DIATONIC SEVENTH CHORDS IN MAJOR KEYS

Just as each scale degree in a major key produces a diatonic triad with a fixed quality, each scale degree also produces a diatonic seventh chord with a fixed quality. The pattern below applies to any major key.

Degree Roman numeral Quality Example in C major
1 Ima7 Major 7 Cma7
2 iimi7 Minor 7 Dmi7
3 iiimi7 Minor 7 Emi7
4 IVma7 Major 7 Fma7
5 ★ V7 Dominant 7 G7 (most important)
6 vimi7 Minor 7 Ami7
7 viiø7 Half-dim 7 Bø7
🎼 PART 5 · BUILDING SEVENTH CHORDS

To build a seventh chord from any root, follow the major key method:

  1. Write the root.
  2. Draw the snowperson: add notes a generic third, fifth, and seventh above the root (alternating lines/spaces).
  3. Apply the major key signature of the root. This gives you the major-major seventh chord (ma7).
  4. Adjust for other qualities: for dominant 7, lower the seventh a half step; for minor 7, lower both the third and seventh; for half-dim, lower the third, fifth, and seventh; for fully diminished, lower the third, fifth, and lower the seventh by a full step.

📝 NOTEBOOK CHALLENGE: BUILD A G7 CHORD
In your notebook, build a G dominant seventh chord (G7) step by step using the major key method. Step 1: write G as the root. Step 2: add the generic third, fifth, and seventh above it (B, D, F). Step 3: apply G major's key signature -- this gives you G-B-D-F♯ (Gma7). Step 4: lower the seventh a half step. What does it become? Write all four note names before opening the check below.
Check your G7 chord.
G7 = G – B – D – F

Starting from G major: G-B-D-F♯ is Gma7 (major-major seventh). Lower F♯ by a half step to F♮ and you have G-B-D-F: the dominant seventh chord (major-minor seventh).

The tritone in G7 is between B (scale degree 7 in C major, the leading tone) and F (scale degree 4 in C major, the subdominant). This tritone wants to resolve: B moves up a half step to C, and F moves down a half step to E. That is why G7 pulls so powerfully toward C major.

💪 THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER

A seventh chord stacks four notes in thirds: root, third, fifth, and seventh. The five qualities are ma7 (bright), 7/dominant (tense, resolves to I), mi7 (dark/smooth), half-dim (uneasy), and fully dim (very tense). The dominant seventh chord (V7) is the most powerful because its tritone resolves outward to the tonic. V7-I is the strongest resolution in tonal music.
