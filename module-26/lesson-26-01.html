LESSON 26.01
The Song Master: Franz Schubert and the Art Song
Lesson
📖 TODAY'S FOCUS
How can a single song, just one voice and a piano, tell a whole story?
Franz Schubert wrote more than 600 songs in a life that lasted only 31 years. In his hands, a poem, a singer, and a piano became a tiny drama full of characters, scenery, and feeling. The kind of song he perfected is the direct ancestor of the songs you stream today. This lesson introduces the composer and the art form he made famous: the Lied.

🎵 PART 1 · MEET FRANZ SCHUBERT
⚡ QUICK CHECK-IN
Think of a song you love. Besides the singer's voice, what else is going on in the background that helps set the mood? (No wrong answer.)

Franz Schubert was born in Vienna in 1797 and died there in 1828, at just 31 years old. In that short life he composed an astonishing amount of music: symphonies, chamber works, piano pieces, and above all more than 600 songs. He admired Beethoven, who lived in the same city, and the two were buried near each other. Schubert's music sits right on the border between two eras. He grew up on the balanced, elegant forms of the Classical period, but his gift for melody and deep feeling points straight ahead into the Romantic period.

WHERE SCHUBERT SITS ON THE TIMELINE
Classical
about 1750–1820
Haydn, Mozart, early Beethoven
Schubert
1797–1828
The bridge: Classical forms, Romantic feeling
Romantic
about 1820–1900
Chopin, Brahms, later composers
Schubert lived at the exact hinge between the two eras, which is why his music can sound both orderly and deeply emotional.
💡 A FEW WORKS TO KNOW
Beyond his songs, Schubert wrote the Unfinished Symphony (Symphony No. 8), the Trout Quintet, and many piano dances. But it is the songs that changed music history. Two of the most famous are Erlkonig (The Erlking) and Gretchen am Spinnrade (Gretchen at the Spinning Wheel).
🎹 PART 2 · WHAT IS A LIED (ART SONG)?

A Lied (say "leet"; the plural is Lieder, say "LEE-der") is a German art song: a poem set to music for a solo singer and piano. The word simply means "song." What made Schubert's Lieder special is that the piano is not just backup. It is an equal partner that paints the scene, sets the mood, and sometimes even acts like a character in the story.

THE TWO PARTNERS OF A LIED
🎤
The Voice
Sings the poem. Carries the melody and delivers the words and the story.
+
🎹
The Piano
Paints the scene and the mood. Not just backup: it can imitate wind, water, hoofbeats, or heartbreak.
=
📜
A Lied
A poem turned into a complete little drama for voice and piano.

Because the poem drives everything, the composer's job is to match the music to the words: the rise and fall of the melody, the harmony, and the piano's figures all serve the meaning of the text.

🎼 PART 3 · WORD-PAINTING: WHEN MUSIC IMITATES THE WORDS

Word-painting is when the music imitates the meaning of the words. Schubert was a master of it, especially in the piano part. Here are his two most famous examples.

Erlkonig (The Erlking)
A father rides through the night on horseback, carrying his sick child. The piano hammers out fast, repeated triplets from beginning to end. You can hear the galloping hooves and the panic without anyone saying a word.
Gretchen am Spinnrade
A young woman sits at her spinning wheel, thinking of someone she loves. The piano plays a circling, whirring figure that never stops: it is the spinning wheel turning, right there in the music.
THE PIANO'S ENGINE IN "ERLKONIG": NONSTOP TRIPLETS
beat:
1 (trip-let)
2 (trip-let)
3 (trip-let)
4 (trip-let)
…
Three quick notes on every beat, over and over, with no rest. That relentless pulse is the galloping horse. This is word-painting.

Listen for these figures. As you play each recording, ask yourself: what is the piano imitating right now?

🎹 PART 4 · THE LIED AND MODERN SONGS

Strip a Schubert Lied down to its bones and you find something very familiar: a singer telling a story, with an instrument backing them up. That is exactly what a modern pop, folk, or country song is. The Lied is the great-great-grandparent of the songs you know. The clearest link is form.

Strophic form
Verse 1
music A Verse 2
music A Verse 3
music A
The same music repeats for every verse. This is how most pop and folk songs work.
Through-composed form
Part 1
music A Part 2
music B Part 3
music C
New music the whole way through, following the story. Erlkonig works this way.

Schubert used both forms. A gentle, folk-like Lied is usually strophic. A dramatic story-song like Erlkonig is through-composed so the music can change as the plot builds. When you notice a modern song repeating the same tune for each verse, you are hearing strophic form, the same idea Schubert used 200 years ago.

💪 THE ONE THING TO REMEMBER

Franz Schubert turned the simple song into a small drama for voice and piano, called a Lied. The piano became a partner that could paint the words, from a galloping horse to a spinning wheel. Every time you hear a singer tell a story over a repeating verse, you are hearing the family tree that runs straight back to Schubert.
